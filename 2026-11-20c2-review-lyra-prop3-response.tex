\section{$\delta_2$ from scratch (proved + computed)}\label{sec:d2}
I proposed $\delta_2$ on 11-19, which makes me the worst-placed reviewer of it. So below I derive it again without looking at Prop.~2 of 11-19, write down every assumption, and then attack it with a lesion. The independent check, by someone other than its author, still has to come from you (\S\ref{sec:trust}).

\textbf{Setting, read from \texttt{a5230bf}.} Block $m$, stratum $k$, and live cell $s_1<\dots<s_n$ with $n\ge2$. The cyclic partner is $t(s_r)=s_{\sigma(r)}$ with $\sigma(r)=r+1\bmod n$. The increment is $X_{m,k}=\frac1n\sum_rW_i(s_r)\big(W_j(s_r)-W_j(s_{\sigma r})\big)-\delta_{m,k}$, with $\delta_{m,k}=2\,\rho_i\rho_j$ where $\rho_\bullet=(\max-\min)/2$ of $p_\bullet$ over the cell. That equals $\tfrac12R_iR_j$, so the code computes what the PDF states.
Dead cells contribute $0$. $b=1+\max_{m,k}\delta_{m,k}$ over the whole stream, and $\Fc_{m-1}=\sigma(\theta,\ \text{outcomes of blocks}<m)$.

\begin{proposition}[second-order slack]\label{prop:d2}
Assume (H1) given $\theta$, outcomes of distinct items are independent; (H2) given $\theta$, $W_i(s)\perp W_j(s)$ with means $p_i(s),p_j(s)$; (H3) $\delta$ is computed from the true $p$-ranges of the cell. Then for every live cell
\[
\E[X_{m,k}\mid\Fc_{m-1}]=\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_r\big(p_i(s_r)-c\big)\big(p_j(s_r)-p_j(s_{\sigma r})\big)-\delta_{m,k}\le\tfrac12R_iR_j-\delta_{m,k}=0\quad(\text{any }c\in\mathbb R).
\]
The bound is attained when $n=2$ and $p_i,p_j$ are comonotone on the cell, so no smaller cell-uniform multiple of $R_iR_j$ is valid.
Moreover every factor satisfies $1+\langle\lambda_m,X_m\rangle\ge1-\frac{1/2+\max\delta}{1+\max\delta}>0$ whenever $\lambda_m\ge0$ and $\sum_k\lambda_{m,k}\le1/b$. Hence $\prod_m(1+\langle\lambda_m,X_m\rangle)$ is a nonnegative supermartingale for every $\Fc_{m-1}$-measurable such $\lambda$, and Ville gives $P(\sup\ge1/\alpha)\le\alpha$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Block $m$'s outcomes are independent of $\Fc_{m-1}$ given $\theta$ by (H1).
$\E[W_i(s)W_j(s)]=p_i(s)p_j(s)$ by (H2). For $t\ne s$, $\E[W_i(s)W_j(t)]=p_i(s)p_j(t)$ by (H1), and $t(s_r)\ne s_r$ because $n\ge2$.
$\sigma$ is a bijection of $\{1..n\}$, so $\sum_r(p_j(s_r)-p_j(s_{\sigma r}))=0$, and subtracting any constant $c$ from $p_i$ leaves the sum unchanged.
Take $c$ to be the midrange of $p_i$ on the cell. Then $|p_i-c|\le R_i/2$ and $|p_j(s_r)-p_j(s_{\sigma r})|\le R_j$, and summing over $n$ terms and dividing by $n$ gives $\tfrac12R_iR_j$.
For equality, let $n=2$, so $\sigma$ is the swap. The mean is $\tfrac12(p_i(s_1)-p_i(s_2))(p_j(s_1)-p_j(s_2))$, which is $\tfrac12R_iR_j$ when the two differences have the same sign.
Factor bound: the raw cell mean lies in $[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$ (11-19 Prop.~3, exhaustive for $n\le7$ and proved for all $n$ by counting cyclic ascents). So $X_{m,k}\ge-\tfrac12-\max\delta$. With $\lambda\ge0$ and $\sum\lambda\le1/b$ the claimed lower bound follows, and linearity gives $\E[\langle\lambda_m,X_m\rangle\mid\Fc_{m-1}]=\sum_k\lambda_{m,k}\E[X_{m,k}\mid\Fc_{m-1}]\le0$.
\end{proof}
\textbf{What the code enforces.} No clipping is needed and none is relied on. Every bettor in \texttt{build\_lambda} assigns only $0$ or a nonnegative share of cap$=1/b$, and \texttt{replay} renormalises when several strata fire.
\texttt{wealth\_path} asserts factor $\ge-10^{-9}$ and \emph{then} applies \texttt{np.maximum(.,0)}. That guard never binds: the measured minimum factor is $0.4999$, which is the $\tfrac12$ of the proof.
One point of scope: $b$ uses the whole-stream $\max\delta$. That is legitimate only because $\theta$ is in $\Fc_0$ (oracle simulation), as on 11-19. Here $\max\delta_2\le0.00125$, so $b=1.00125$ and the point is now cosmetic.

\section{Your (b): Regime~2: keep top-3 as a diagnostic, promote the mixture (computed)}
\textbf{Why top-3 is a diagnostic.} Regime~2 puts the shock in the top-30\% strata by construction. The constant bettor on strata 7--9 is fixed before the \emph{data}, so it is valid, but it is not fixed before the \emph{alternative}: its weights are the alternative's support.
Its power ($0.80/0.998$ at $\gamma=0.20/0.35$ here) is therefore an \emph{oracle-support} power. It shows the envelope contains Regime~2, which is what my retraction needed, and it says nothing about what a deployer would achieve.
Note also that no $\theta$-only prior points there. The Marshall--Olkin signal scales as $(1-p_i)(1-p_j)$, which is \emph{smallest} in the hard strata, so a ``prior from the signal formula'' would weight the wrong end.

\textbf{What is worth a proposition.} An average of e-processes on one filtration is an e-process, so any mixture fixed in advance is valid whatever the alternative. I ran two of them (\texttt{mixture\_top3.py}, my 11-19 engine, $\delta_2$ cyclic, $R=500$, seeds $1000+r$).
\textsc{Mix} is $\tfrac12(\textsc{Eq}+\textsc{Top3})$. \textsc{MixWin} gives equal prior to \textsc{Eq} and the eight contiguous three-stratum windows $\{k,k+1,k+2\}$, nine components, and does \emph{not} know where the shock is. It does assume co-failure is contiguous in difficulty, which is a structural prior to state.

\textsc{MixWin} takes Regime~2 from $0.03$ to $0.86$ at $\gamma=0.35$ without being told the support, and it is valid by construction.
That is the result I would state, once you reproduce it in your harness, with Regime-1 power for \textsc{MixWin} to show what it costs where equal weights are already right (I have not run that), and Type-I on the latent and drift nulls (a formality given validity, but it checks the code).
It also answers your note's third question: yes, but use a \emph{mixture} over plausible supports, not a single difficulty-concentrated prior.
It connects to your ONS-type learner as well. A finite mixture is the Bayesian counterpart of learning the weights, and here it beats equal weights by a wide margin, which is the opposite of the Regime-1 puzzle. That contrast would itself be a clean sentence for the note.
