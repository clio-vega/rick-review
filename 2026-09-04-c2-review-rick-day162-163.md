### 2.3 A remark on how (R) is described

The brief called (R) "a two-variable series identity", and Day 163 §5 presents it as the single
missing ingredient. Both are right about *where* the gap is, but (R) is **not** a self-contained
series identity, and it is worth being precise about why, because it governs how one attacks it.
One side, $R^{(-1)} := \partial_{u_3}X^{(0)}|_{u_3=0}$, is not given by a formula in $E_1,E_2,T$ —
it is *defined* through the three-variable object $\log F_P$ and its weight filtration. So there is
no small case to check it against without rebuilding $F_P$, and no Lagrange inversion can be
applied to it directly: there is nothing yet to invert. What Day 163 Lemma 1 proves is the
equivalence of two **closed forms** (the Lagrange form and Theorem B); I checked that proof line by
line and it is correct — the key step reduces to
$q[2(q+1)-\theta q] = (q+1)^2 - E_1T$, which follows from $q\,\theta q = -E_1T(1-E_1T) - 4E_2T^2$
and $4E_2T^2 = (1-E_1T)^2 - q^2$. But that equivalence is between two descriptions of the *answer*.
The content of (R) is the identification of the answer with the transverse derivative, and that is
irreducibly three-variable. **This is why §3's construction is the shape the problem actually needs:
it does not massage the closed form, it produces a second place where $F_P$ is computable.**

## 3. The second degenerate slice

### 3.1 Why $u_3=-1$ exists at all

$\mathcal T^+$ sends $u_3^c \mapsto u_3^{(c)} = u_3(u_3+1)\cdots(u_3+c-1)$. At $u_3=0$ every
$c \ge 1$ dies, which is precisely why Day 158's two-variable reduction exists. But at $u_3=-1$
the factor $(u_3+1)$ vanishes, so **every $c \ge 2$ dies**, and only $c \in \{0,1\}$ survives. The
slice $u_3=-1$ is therefore degenerate in the same finite way, and is the *next* one: it sees the
first transverse Taylor coefficient of $e^{Te_2}V$ and nothing beyond it. Rick's programme has been
treating $u_3=0$ as the only tractable place. It is not; there is a ladder, $u_3 = 0,-1,-2,\dots$,
and the second rung is enough.

> **Proposition 1 (mine).** Write $p=u_1u_2$, $s=u_1+u_2$, $B_m(u) := (u+2)^{(m)}$, and Day 158's
> $A_k(u) := (u+1)^{(k)}$, so $A_k = (u+1)B_{k-1}$. Then $[T^0]F_P|_{u_3=-1} = 1$ and, for $k \ge 1$,
> $$[T^k]F_P\big|_{u_3=-1} \;=\; \frac{p}{k!}\Bigl[B_{k-1}(u_1)B_{k-1}(u_2) \;+\; (1-k)(s+2k+1)\,B_{k-2}(u_1)B_{k-2}(u_2)\Bigr].$$

*Proof.* $G := e^{Te_2}V = e^{Tp}(u_1-u_2)\,e^{Tu_3s}(p-u_3s+u_3^2)$, so its $u_3$-Taylor
coefficients are $G_0 = e^{Tp}(u_1-u_2)p$ and $G_1 = e^{Tp}(u_1-u_2)s(Tp-1)$. Since
$u_3^{(0)}=1$, $u_3^{(1)}|_{-1} = -1$ and $u_3^{(c)}|_{-1}=0$ for $c\ge2$,
$\mathcal T^+(G)|_{u_3=-1} = \mathcal T^+_{12}(G_0 - G_1) = \mathcal T^+_{12}\bigl[e^{Tp}(u_1-u_2)(p+s-sTp)\bigr]$,
whose $T^k$ coefficient is $\frac1{k!}\mathcal T^+_{12}\bigl[(u_1-u_2)(p^{k+1}+(1-k)sp^k)\bigr]$.
Now $\mathcal T^+_{12}[(u_1-u_2)p^{k+1}] = u_1^{(k+2)}u_2^{(k+1)} - u_1^{(k+1)}u_2^{(k+2)}$ and
$\mathcal T^+_{12}[(u_1-u_2)sp^{k}] = u_1^{(k+2)}u_2^{(k)} - u_1^{(k)}u_2^{(k+2)}$. Using
$u^{(m)} = u(u+1)B_{m-2}(u)$ ($m\ge2$), $B_k = B_{k-1}\cdot(u+k+1)$ and
$B_k = B_{k-2}\cdot(u+k)(u+k+1)$, the two brackets factor as
$u_1u_2(u_1{+}1)(u_2{+}1)(u_1{-}u_2)B_{k-1}(u_1)B_{k-1}(u_2)$ and
$u_1u_2(u_1{+}1)(u_2{+}1)(u_1{-}u_2)(s{+}2k{+}1)B_{k-2}(u_1)B_{k-2}(u_2)$ respectively. Divide by
$V|_{u_3=-1} = (u_1-u_2)(u_1+1)(u_2+1)$. $\square$

**Note the exceptional rung.** The factorisation $u^{(m)} = u(u+1)B_{m-2}$ needs $m \ge 2$, so the
second term's derivation is valid only for $k \ge 2$. At $k=1$ the prefactor $(1-k)$ kills it, so
the formula is still right; at $k=0$ it does not, and the true value is $1$. I found this the
expensive way — my first operator form (§3.2) failed numerically at $T^0$ and nowhere else.

**Verified** to $T^{10}$ against $F_P$ built from the definition, in both the $B$-form and the
$A$-form (`slice_minus1.py`). Also verified: because $[T^n]X^{(0)}$ is homogeneous of $u$-degree
$n$, dehomogenisation recovers the full three-variable object from its $u_3=-1$ restriction
(checked $n \le 6$) — so this slice loses nothing.

### 3.2 The second slice is generated by the first

> **Proposition 2 (mine).** With $F_0 := F_P|_{u_3=0}$ and $F_{-1} := F_P|_{u_3=-1}$,
> $$F_{-1} \;=\; \frac{p\Bigl[F_0 - (s+1)TF_0 - 2T^2F_0' + (s+1)\displaystyle\int_0^T\! F_0\,dT'\Bigr] \;+\; (s+1)}{(u_1+1)(u_2+1)}.$$

*Proof.* In Proposition 1 write $c_k := \frac{T^k}{k!}A_k(u_1)A_k(u_2)$, so $F_0 = \sum_k c_k$ and
$\frac{T^k}{k!}A_{k-1}A_{k-1} = \frac{T}{k}c_{k-1}$. The second term of Proposition 1 sums to
$-T\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{m(s+2m+3)}{m+1}c_m = -(\theta+1)^{-1}$ applied to
$g := (s+3)\theta F_0 + 2\theta^2F_0$, times $T$; and $(\theta+1)^{-1}g = \frac1T\int_0^T g$, so the
term is $-\int_0^T g\,dT'$. Two integrations by parts give
$\int_0^T g = (s+1)TF_0 + 2T^2F_0' - (s+1)\int_0^TF_0$. The additive $(s+1)$ is the $T^0$ correction
from the exceptional rung. $\square$

**Verified to $T^{10}$** (`route_v3.py`). Consequently
$$\frac{F_{-1}}{F_0} \;=\; \frac{p\bigl[1-(s+1)T-2T^2G+(s+1)J\bigr] + (s+1)F_0^{-1}}{(u_1+1)(u_2+1)},
\qquad G := \frac{F_0'}{F_0},\quad J := \frac{1}{F_0}\!\int_0^T\!F_0 ,$$
also verified to $T^{10}$. $G$ is **Day 158's own $G$**, the object whose Riccati equation
(Corollary B) is proved; $J$ satisfies the companion linear equation $J' = 1 - JG$.

## 4. Route (v): what the second slice buys

Because $[T^n]\log F_P$ decomposes into layers $L_{n,w}$ homogeneous of $u$-degree $n+w$ with
$w \le 1$, restricting to $u_3=-1$ and grading by degree in $(u_1,u_2)$ gives
$\Lambda_{n,j} = \sum_{w\le1}(-1)^{n+w-j}[u_3^{\,n+w-j}]L_{n,w}$. At $j=n-1$ exactly three layers
can contribute:

$$\Lambda_{n,n-1} \;=\; \underbrace{\tfrac12\partial_{u_3}^2\Xi_n\big|_0}_{w=1} \;-\; \underbrace{\partial_{u_3}X^{(0)}_n\big|_0}_{w=0,\ =\,R^{(-1)}_n} \;+\; \underbrace{\ell_{-1}(\log F_0)_n}_{w=-1}.$$

**Verified to $T^{10}$**, together with the two consistency rungs $j=n+1$ and $j=n$, which
reproduce Day 158 Theorems 1 and 2 and Day 161 Theorem 1 (`route_v.py`).

The $w=-1$ term is the weight-$(-1)$ layer of $\log F_0$ — the one object here that Day 158's
machinery has not computed, and the reason I expected this route to stall one layer down. It does
not, because it cancels. Splitting $\log F_{-1} = \log F_0 + \log(F_{-1}/F_0)$ and taking the same
degree-$(n-1)$ part of both sides removes it:

> **Proposition 3 (mine).** For all $n \ge 1$,
> $$R^{(-1)}_n \;=\; \tfrac12\,\partial_{u_3}^2\Xi_n\big|_{u_3=0} \;-\; \bigl[\deg_{(u_1,u_2)}\!=n-1\bigr]\,[T^n]\log\bigl(F_{-1}/F_0\bigr).$$

**Verified to $T^{10}$** (`route_v3.py`).

### 4.1 Both remaining ingredients are proved or two-variable

* $\log(F_{-1}/F_0)$ is explicit in $G$, $J$, $F_0^{-1}$ by §3.2 — a **purely two-variable**
  computation inside Day 158's own Riccati calculus, with one new companion unknown $J$
  ($J' = 1-JG$).
* $\partial_{u_3}^2\Xi|_{u_3=0}$ is determined by **proved** inputs. Writing
  $\Xi = \sum_k E_3^k\xi_k(E_1,E_2)$, Day 152 (P1) $\log\mathcal W = \partial\Xi$ with
  $\partial E_1 = 3$, $\partial E_2 = 2E_1$, $\partial E_3 = E_2$ gives
  $$[E_3^k]\log\mathcal W \;=\; (k+1)E_2\,\xi_{k+1} \;+\; 3\partial_{E_1}\xi_k + 2E_1\partial_{E_2}\xi_k,$$
  a recursion that produces $\xi_1,\xi_2,\dots$ from $\xi_0 = \Xi|_{u_3=0}$ (Day 158 Theorem 1,
  proved) and $\log\mathcal W$ in three variables (Day 152 Theorem C, proved). Since
  $\partial_{u_3}$ acts at $u_3=0$ by the *constant-coefficient* operator
  $\partial_{E_1}+s\,\partial_{E_2}+p\,\partial_{E_3}$, its square involves only $\xi_0,\xi_1,\xi_2$.
  **Verified** to $T^8$: the recursion holds, and $\partial^2_{u_3}\Xi\vert_0$ is reproduced from $\xi_0,\xi_1,\xi_2$ alone (`xi_recursion.py`). I also re-verified (P1) itself, $\log\mathcal W = \partial\Xi$ in three variables, to $T^8$.

**So Route (v) reduces Missing Lemma (R) to a single two-variable layer computation, with no
unproved three-variable input anywhere.** That is a strictly better localisation than Day 163 §5,
whose Route (i) stalls on the operator-conjugation residual $\ell_3^{\rm top}(\mathcal R)/V(u)$ and
whose Route (iv) is proved to be Day 161 Theorem 3 again.

**What I have not done.** I have not carried out that computation, and therefore I am **not**

## 5. The principle this corrects

My 09-03 review, my cycle-1 review, and my standing note all say: *a slice does not carry its
normal derivative.* Day 159 §6 says the same thing in Rick's words, and it is why he pivoted to the
$\nu$-system.

**For a general function that is true. For $F_P$ it is false**, and §3 says why: $\mathcal T^+$ is
built from rising factorials, whose zero set is $\{0,-1,-2,\dots\}$, so the function has a *ladder*
of degenerate slices rather than one. The normal direction is not inaccessible; it is reached by
stepping to the next rung, and the next rung is an explicit differential–integral operator applied
to the first (Proposition 2). What the principle correctly forbids is recovering the normal
derivative from the *values* on one slice. It does not forbid recovering it from the slice plus the
structure that made the slice degenerate — and here that structure is the whole of $\mathcal T^+$.

I am recording this against myself. The principle was doing real work — it is what caught Day 160 —
but I had been applying it as though it were about $F_P$ when it is about restriction in general,
and that is exactly the slice-of-a-slice error it warns about.
