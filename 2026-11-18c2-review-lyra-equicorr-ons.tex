\section{Q1: is there a finite-sample lower bound on $\bar W(\mathrm{Fixed})-\bar W(\mathrm{Online})$?}
\paragraph{What is proved: an exact decomposition.}
Let $\ell(\lambda)=\E\log(1+\langle\lambda,X\rangle)$ per block, let $\lambda^\ast=\arg\max_K\ell$ (the population Kelly point), and let $D(\lambda)=\ell(\lambda^\ast)-\ell(\lambda)\ge0$. For stationary blocks and $M$ blocks,
\[
\E W_{\rm Fixed}-\E W_{\rm Online}\;=\;\underbrace{\big(M\ell(\lambda^\ast)-\E W_{\rm Online}\big)}_{\text{learner's cost }C_{\rm on}}\;-\;\underbrace{M\,D(\lambda_F)}_{\text{Fixed's bias}} .
\]
This is the forecast-combination puzzle in the bias-versus-estimation-variance form of Smith--Wallis and Claeskens et al.\ (references below), transposed to log-wealth. Equal weights win exactly when $M D(\lambda_F)<C_{\rm on}$.
\paragraph{What is proved: the Hessian identity behind $\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_{\hat w}H)$.}
For $f(\lambda)=-\log(1+\langle\lambda,X\rangle)$ we have $\nabla f=-X/(1+\langle\lambda,X\rangle)$ and $\nabla^2 f=XX^\top/(1+\langle\lambda,X\rangle)^2=\nabla f\nabla f^\top$, \emph{identically in $\lambda$}. This is exp-concavity with $\alpha=1$; your code's $\alpha=\tfrac12$ is valid but loose. At an interior $\lambda^\ast$ it gives $H=\E[gg^\top]=\Cov(g)$. The usual second-order heuristic for a plug-in learner, $C_{\rm on}\approx\sum_m\tfrac12\mathrm{tr}(H\,\Cov\hat\lambda_m)$, therefore needs no unknown constants: it is $\approx\tfrac k2\log M$, where $k$ is the number of free coordinates. That is a heuristic, not a bound.
\paragraph{What is \emph{not} available: a per-DGP lower bound for this learner.}
Regret theory gives \emph{upper} bounds on $C_{\rm on}$. For a correctly tuned ONS, Hazan's Theorem~\texttt{thm:onsregret} gives $2(1/\alpha+GD)\,Q\log M\approx 1.3\times10^3$ nats at your constants, which is vacuous at $M=200$ (\emph{read}: arXiv:1909.05207, \S``The Online Newton Step Algorithm''). The only lower bounds I know are \emph{minimax}. Rewrite the factor as a portfolio over $Q+1$ assets (cash, plus asset $s$ with gross return $1+X_s/b$, weights $w_s=b\lambda_s$). For constant-rebalanced portfolios, Ordentlich--Cover show that \emph{any} learner pays $\tfrac Q2\log M+O(1)$ against the hindsight-best portfolio on its worst sequence. That bound is over adversarial sequences and says nothing about a given stochastic DGP. So my answer is: \textbf{yes as an identity, no as a bound}. What you can publish is the decomposition, together with $M D(\lambda_F)$ computed for the DGP and $C_{\rm on}$ measured.

\section{F2: Regime 1 is not equicorrelated (proved + computed)}
\begin{proposition}[the statistic's signal]\label{prop:signal}
In a stratum with marginal rates $p_i,p_j$ (constant up to $\varepsilon$) and Marshall--Olkin shock probability $\gamma$, take items $s\neq t$ with independent outcomes, so that $W_j(t)$ is \emph{not} conditioned on. Then
$\E[U_s-V_s]=\gamma(1-\gamma)(1-p_i)(1-p_j)$, up to the within-stratum $\pm2\varepsilon$ term.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\E U_s=\gamma+(1-\gamma)p_ip_j$, and $\E V_s=\E W_i(s)\,\E W_j(t)=(\gamma+(1-\gamma)p_i)(\gamma+(1-\gamma)p_j)$. Subtracting gives $\gamma(1-\gamma)\,[1-p_i-p_j+p_ip_j]$.
\end{proof}
So a ``uniform'' shock produces signal that falls off with difficulty. At $\gamma=0.25$ the measured $\bar X_s$ runs from $+0.091$ (easiest) down to $-0.014$ (hardest). (The proposition predicts $0.102$ and $-0.012$ after subtracting $\delta=0.05$; the shortfall in the easiest strata is the $\approx12\%$ of blocks where a stratum has no valid item and $X_{s,m}=0$.) The population Kelly point is
$\lambda^\ast/\mathrm{cap}=(0.60,\,0.31,\,0.10,\,0,\dots,0)$ (\texttt{lamstar\_g025.txt}). Equal weights spend 70\% of the budget on strata 3--9, which the Kelly point leaves at zero (their drifts run from $+0.051$ down to $-0.014$). Regime~1 therefore tests ``learner vs.\ a biased fixed point''. That is a different question from the puzzle's ``learner vs.\ the optimal fixed point''. To test the puzzle as stated, use a DGP in which $\lambda^\ast$ really is uniform: for example, scale the per-stratum shock probability so that $\gamma_s(1-\gamma_s)(1-p_i)(1-p_j)$ is constant in $s$.

\section{Q2 and F3: validity under past-only pairing}
Your Lemma (my 11-13 note, Lemma~6: Vovk--Wang joint linear bet) needs $\E[X_{s,m}\mid\Fc_{m-1}]\le0$ for every $s$. The rest of your question, about cross-stratum correlation from the shared ramp, is then harmless, \emph{provided} that hypothesis holds. The cross-stratum correlation cannot break validity under that hypothesis. The hypothesis itself is what fails.
\begin{proposition}[past-only pairing is not a conditional e-value]\label{prop:fail}
Let $\Fc_{s-1}$ contain the outcomes of items $<s$ and the partner $t(s)<s$, as your docstring requires (``$t(s)$ is chosen before item $s$ is observed''). Under the static null,
\[\E[U_s-V_s-\delta\mid\Fc_{s-1},\theta_s]=p_i(\theta_s)\big(p_j(\theta_s)-W_j(t(s))\big)-\delta,\]
which equals $p_ip_j-\delta>0$ on $\{W_j(t(s))=0\}$ whenever $p_ip_j>\delta$. In strata $\ge5$, $p_ip_j\ge0.105>\delta\approx0.05$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$W_j(t(s))$ is $\Fc_{s-1}$-measurable, so $\E[W_i(s)W_j(t(s))\mid\Fc_{s-1},\theta_s]=p_i(\theta_s)W_j(t(s))$. If $t(s)$ is hidden until time $s$, $W_j(t(s))$ is replaced by the realised past same-stratum mean, which is still $\Fc_{s-1}$-measurable and still random.
\end{proof}
\textbf{Counterexample (computed, \texttt{validity.py}).} Use your block joint bet with $\lambda_{s,m}=\mathrm{cap}/\#\{\text{chosen}\}$ on strata $s\ge5$ whose block items all have partners with $W_j(t)=0$, and $0$ otherwise. This is predictable and has $\sum_s\lambda_{s,m}\le1/b$, so it is in your admissible class. Under \texttt{null\_dgp} it gives \textbf{Type-I $=1.000$} ($R=1000$, mean $\log W=+15.98$).
Fixed and ONS do not read $W_j(t)$, which is why their Type-I stays at $0$. But ONS reads past $X$, which is correlated with past $W_j$, so its validity is not covered by any theorem either.
\begin{proposition}[fresh pairs restore the hypothesis]\label{prop:fresh}
Pair each item with a same-stratum item of the \emph{same block}, so that neither outcome is in $\Fc_{m-1}$; the strata of the block are known beforehand, since $\theta$ is. Assume the null: distinct items are independent given $\theta$. Then $\E[W_i(s)(W_j(s)-W_j(t))\mid\Fc_{m-1},\theta]=p_i(s)(p_j(s)-p_j(t))\le p_i(s)\cdot2\varepsilon_{k}\le\delta_k$. Hence $\E[X_{k,m}\mid\Fc_{m-1}]\le0$, and the joint linear bet is a test supermartingale for \emph{every} predictable $\lambda$, ONS included, under arbitrary cross-stratum dependence.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The expectation of an average is linear, so no independence \emph{between} pairs is needed, and pairs may overlap (I used a cyclic pairing). The bound is $|p_j(s)-p_j(t)|\le\mathrm{range}=2\varepsilon_k$ and $p_i\le1$.
\end{proof}
Under the same upper-strata bettor, the fresh-pair design gives Type-I $0.000$ ($R=1000$). It costs bets: with BLOCK $=20$ and $Q=10$, a stratum has $\ge2$ items in a block with probability $0.61$.
\textbf{The drift null is not a stress test as built.} \texttt{stratum\_epsilon} takes the $p$-range over the \emph{whole stream}, so it already contains the full $0.25$ ramp. Under drift, $\delta_s\in[0.27,0.29]$ against $0.05$ static, so $b=1.29$ and Fixed decays at $-39.8$ nats. Type-I $=0$ is bought by an oracle slack that knows the future ramp. With fresh pairs, $\delta$ only needs to cover the range within one block and stratum: the ramp adds $0.25\cdot20/4000=0.00125$, so $\delta\approx0.05$ even under drift.
\textbf{Recommendation:} use fresh-pair pairing; recompute $\delta$ per block; re-run Regime~3. Until then, the scope caveat should say ``Type-I is Monte Carlo evidence for the Fixed and ONS bettors; the supermartingale argument does not apply to past-only pairing''.

\section{Q3: Regime 2 is outside the envelope of the statistic (proved + computed)}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:signal}, a shock in the hardest strata ($p\approx0.45$--$0.6$) yields signal $\gamma(1-\gamma)(1-p_i)(1-p_j)\approx0.2\,\gamma(1-\gamma)$, which is at most $0.05$, and the slack is $\delta\approx0.05$. The signal does not grow past $\gamma=\tfrac12$. Population Kelly reaches $M\ell^\ast=+0.01,+0.23,+0.47$ nats at $\gamma=0.25,0.35,0.6$, against $\ln20=3.00$. The \emph{population}-weighted fixed bet has power $0.000$, $0.000$, $0.010$ (\texttt{r2\_envelope.txt}).
So no valid weighting detects Regime~2, and pushing to $\gamma\gtrsim0.6$ will not change that.
Your ``Oracle'' is \emph{not} population-optimal, contrary to PDF \S1. It is the best constant in hindsight, fitted to each run's realised stream (\texttt{oracle\_weights}), so it is not a valid test, and its $0.18$ power at $\gamma=0.35$ is selection.
The scope sentence I would write: \emph{the cross-item co-failure statistic is blind to co-failure concentrated on items both judges usually fail.} For LLM judges, that may be where co-failure matters most, so I think this deserves a sentence in the paper rather than a footnote.

\section{Smaller points}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{ONS as implemented is not Hazan's ONS.} \texttt{thm:onsregret} requires $\gamma=\tfrac12\min\{1/(GD),\alpha\}$ ($\approx0.05$ here), $\varepsilon=1/(\gamma^2D^2)$ ($\approx44$), and the $A_t$-norm projection. Its proof uses that norm in its Pythagorean step. The code floors $\gamma$ at $0.25$, sets $\varepsilon=1$, and projects in the Euclidean norm. Call it ``ONS-type'' or align it with the theorem. No regret guarantee applies to the current version.
\item \textbf{Reference [2] is mistitled.} arXiv:2205.04216 is Wang, Hyndman, Li, Kang, \emph{Forecast combinations: an over 50-year review} (\emph{read}: arXiv abstract), not ``Wang \& Hyndman, Forecast combination puzzles and solutions''. For the puzzle itself, the primary sources are Smith--Wallis and Claeskens et al.\ below.
\item A one-dimensional alternative (computed, \texttt{rerun.py}): a uniform mixture of 11 equal-direction bets with scales $c\cdot\mathrm{cap}$, $c\in\{0,0.1,\dots,1\}$, is valid as an average of e-processes and can lose at most $\ln 11$ to its best scale. It reaches $\bar W=-1.70$ vs Fixed $-7.65$ at $\gamma=0.02$, but power $0.85$ vs $0.98$ at $\gamma=0.25$. That is data, not a recommendation.
\end{itemize}
