## 1. Q75: Key Identity, Theorem 1, Corollary 2

### 1.1 Summary (one sentence)

### 1.2 Key Identity: re-derived
- **(L1)–(L3).** Correct. (L2) is the quasi-shuffle length bound. In (L3), each factor of a product of S-linear elements receives at most one part, in order.
- **Odd case.** Write M_α^⊥ U^r = Σ_{p_1<⋯<p_ℓ}…, then sum over r. The pair (r, positions) corresponds bijectively to gap vectors (g_0,…,g_ℓ) ≥ 0, so the sum is W(D_{α_1}U)W⋯(D_{α_ℓ}U)W. Since U excludes S_0, we have D_mU = −z^mΨ for m even and −z^mΘ for m odd. Then WΨW = W collapses the even slots. Nothing here uses commutativity: the odd slots keep their order, and the result (WΘ)^oW does not depend on where the even parts sit. ✔
- **Even case.** In M_α^⊥(WΘ) = (M_α^⊥W)Θ + (M_{α'}^⊥W)(D_mΘ), the two terms cancel for m even, and WΨ = 1 gives the m odd formula. I also checked the edge case ℓ = 1, α' = ∅. ✔
- **Independent check** (`keyid.py`). The LHS M_α^⊥R_s is computed only from the pairing, [R_γ]M_α^⊥R_s = [F_s](M_α F_γ), with M_α expanded in F and products of F's taken as shuffles of permutations. The RHS is built in the *ribbon* basis of NSym, with R_aR_b = R_{a·b} + R_{a⊙b}. **W is obtained as a formal series inverse of Ψ, not from (G-odd)**, and (G-odd) and (G-even) are then checked as outputs (z-degree ≤ 11). So Ω_m = r* ⊗ (r* − u*)^{⊗(m−1)} ⊗ u* becomes (r*+ε*) ⊗ (r*)^{⊗(m−1)} ⊗ ε* in the {r, ε}-dual basis, exactly as you write. In the {r, ε} picture an ε is a block break, and u = ε − r produces the coarsening sum. The windows x r^{m−1} ε are then exactly "one per block of ≥ m cells", which is D_m on S^β. ✔

Here B = H_a ⊗ H_b omits π_a, and every π_i with i ≠ a preserves the number of B's among the first a letters. So the level spans are B-stable and the sum is direct. ✔

### 3.3 (H∞) holds for every weak-Bruhat interval module: a proof
§6 of your note calls (H∞) beyond skew shapes "a hunch". It is a theorem:

> **Proposition.** Let X = B(σ, τ) be any left-weak interval module of H_a(0), decomposable or not, and let 0 ≤ t ≤ a. Then every indecomposable summand of Res_{H_{a−t}} X has a multiplicity-free top. In particular (H) and (H∞) hold for every indecomposable interval module.
>
> *Proof.*
> (i) H_n(0) is split basic: its simples are the 2^{n−1} pairwise non-isomorphic one-dimensional modules C_D (Norton). So every **cyclic** module H/I has top H/(I + J), a quotient of ⊕_D C_D, and is therefore multiplicity-free. The same holds for every direct summand of a cyclic module.
> (ii) An interval module is cyclic, generated by σ. Each γ ∈ [σ, τ]_L is s_{i_k}⋯s_{i_1}σ, length-additively, with every intermediate element in the interval, so π_{i_k}⋯π_{i_1}σ = γ.
> (iii) With J = {1, …, a−t−1}, left multiplication by s_i (i ∈ J) preserves right cosets W_Jg. So Res X = ⊕ over cosets of the spans of [σ, τ] ∩ W_Jg, and each piece is an H_J-submodule.
> (iv) W_Jg = [g_J, w_0(J)g_J] is an interval of the left weak order, by the length-additive factorisation γ = u·g_J. The left weak order is a lattice (Björner; standard, locator not re-checked by me). Hence [σ, τ] ∩ W_Jg = [σ ∨ g_J, τ ∧ w_0(J)g_J] is an interval. On this interval π_i (i ∈ J) acts by the interval-module rule, so by (ii) the piece is cyclic.
> (v) By Krull–Schmidt, the indecomposable summands of Res X are those of the pieces. Apply (i). ∎

**Computation.** `hinf.py` restricts all **1899** intervals at m = 5 (and all 151 at m = 4), for every t with |J| ≥ 2. It finds **0 coset pieces with a repeated top label**, which is exactly what (iv) predicts, and 0 at t = 0. Firing control: on X ⊕ X the same function reports multiplicity 2 for every top label. The decomposition fallback, Fitting splitting along random endomorphisms, was never needed on the corrected modules.

**Consequences for your note.**
- (H) is automatic for every pair of indecomposable interval modules. That explains your observation "all of them have multiplicity-free tops".
- Proposition 6 holds for **all** indecomposable interval modules.
- So the converse of Cor. 5(c) is **proved**, not just computed, for interval modules: L₁(Y) ⇒ Y ≅ Q ⊠ Y′.

I would add the proposition to §1 as a lemma and delete the "hunch" bullet.
