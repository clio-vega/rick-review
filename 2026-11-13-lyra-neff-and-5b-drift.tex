\section{UID 869: Ringelmann, Bay--Yearick, and $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$}

\paragraph{Main claim in one sentence.} Kish's $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=N/(1+(N-1)\rho)$ is the $\beta=0$ member of the Ringelmann family $\rho_N=cN^{-\beta}$, and the $\beta=0$ ceiling $1/\rho$ is shared by three settings.
The algebra is right. I checked the four asymptotic regimes symbolically (\texttt{checks.py}, Q1a).
Both source formulas appear verbatim: \texttt{2606.02646} \S3.1 defines $\hat\rho_N:=(N\cdot A_{\mathrm{pair}}-1)/(N-1)$, and \texttt{2606.28661} \S2, eq.~(icc), gives $\rho=\Corr(Y_i,Y_j)=\Var(\theta)/[s(1-s)]$.

\subsection{The scoping claim inverts the source}
Your \S3 says Ringelmann's $\rho_N$ ``decays with $N$ ($\beta>0$)'' and that ``30 agents $\equiv$ 1'' is a $\beta>0$ statement. The e-print says the opposite (\textbf{read}):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \S5.1 and Table~1: ``Under dense debate, all three tasks sit in the hard-ceiling regime ($\beta\approx0$, $c\in\{0.85,0.57,0.81\}$ on MMLU-Hard, GSM-Hard, GPQA respectively, asymptotic ceilings $1/c\approx1.2$, $1.8$, $1.2$).'' MMLU-Hard debate has $\beta=.000$.
\item The $\beta>0$ fits ($\beta\approx0.25$) are the \emph{non-debate} conditions on GSM-Hard (self-correction, noise, self-consistency), at the answer level only. ``At the correctness level, all GSM-Hard conditions are hard ceiling.''
\item Their abstract and C3: debate ``flattens to $\beta\to0$''. Their mean-field theorem predicts $\beta_{\mathrm{agr}}\approx0$ for fixed peer count. Their abstract also says ``only architectural diversity (heterogeneous teams) lowers $c$''.
\end{itemize}
So ``thirty debating agents $\equiv$ one'' is a $\beta=0$ statement with a large $c$ ($1/c\approx1.2$). Debate is not the decorrelating mechanism that buys $\beta>0$. It is what drives $\beta$ to $0$. Your ``Why this matters'' paragraph lists debate as a decorrelating mechanism and needs the same correction.
Your conclusion that the 30-agent figure cannot be chained to Kohli's 2.18 still holds, but for a different reason: the two papers measure different objects. One is post-communication answer agreement; the other is co-failure of non-communicating judges. Ringelmann itself says (\S3.1) that on answer-level agreement the Kish form ``is a variance-matching definitional rescaling''.
The thesis survives in the source's own words, through $c$ rather than $\beta$: architectural diversity lowers the ceiling parameter.

\subsection{Q1: does $\beta>0$ make $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ merely descriptive?}
\textbf{Answer: no. The line you want does not fall at $\beta=0$ versus $\beta>0$.} Two properties are being merged.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Per-$N$ generativity}: the $N$-panel law is a mixture of i.i.d.\ Bernoulli$(\Theta_N)$.
\item \emph{Projectivity}: one infinite exchangeable sequence has all the $N$-panel laws as its marginals.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{proposition}[proved]\label{prop:proj}
If the exchangeable laws $\{P_N\}$ are projective, then $\rho_N$ is constant in $N$. Hence $\beta>0$ with $c>0$ excludes projectivity.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Under projectivity, the $(1,2)$-marginal of $P_N$ is the $(1,2)$-marginal of $P_2$ for every $N$. The correlation is a function of that pair law alone.
\end{proof}
This is the true core of your Q1. It does \emph{not} make the $\beta>0$ statistic non-generative. \textbf{Counterexample family (computed, exact for $N=2,\dots,7$).} Take $s=\tfrac12$ and $\Theta_N=\tfrac12\pm\tfrac1{4N}$, each with probability $\tfrac12$, so that $Y_i\mid\Theta_N$ are i.i.d.\ Bernoulli. Then $\Var(\Theta_N)=1/(16N^2)$, $\rho_N=\tfrac14N^{-2}$ (that is, $c=\tfrac14$, $\beta=2$), and Kish is exact for $\Var(\bar Y)$. Every $N$ has a latent variable, and $\Var(\Theta_N)$ is generative at that $N$: communication re-draws the latent as the team grows. What fails is (ii). The $N=3$ member's pair marginal is not the $N=2$ member (checked), so the ceiling $1/\rho$ is no longer a statement about drawing more copies from \emph{one} population.

Conversely, \textbf{$\beta=0$ does not supply (i)}. The exchangeable $N=3$ law with $P(S=k)=(\tfrac13,0,\tfrac12,\tfrac16)$, $k=0,\dots,3$, has $s=\tfrac12$ and constant $\rho=\tfrac13>0$. Yet $\E[\Theta]\E[\Theta^3]=\tfrac1{12}<\tfrac19=\E[\Theta^2]^2$ for the moments it would have to have, which contradicts Cauchy--Schwarz. So it is not an i.i.d.\ mixture (computed).
Two further facts help (computed). $\Var(\Theta)$ is \emph{identified} by the pair law whenever a mixture exists. The mixing measure is not: the two laws $\{\tfrac14,\tfrac34\}$ and $\{0{:}\tfrac18,\ \tfrac12{:}\tfrac34,\ 1{:}\tfrac18\}$ give identical $N=2$ laws and different $N=4$ laws. Also, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of the mean depends on second moments only, so ``generative'' is a property of the model you put behind it, never of $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Bay--Yearick name exactly this hypothesis (\S2.1): ``infinite exchangeability, the one further assumption, carries every $n\to\infty$ limit''.
\textbf{Corrected statement:} at $\beta>0$, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}(N)$ can still be read as $\Var(\Theta_N)$ for an $N$-indexed latent, but the $N\to\infty$ ceiling is not a property of any single population. At $\beta=0$ the single-population reading is \emph{allowed}, not implied.

\subsection{Q2: are Bay--Yearick's $\rho$ and your $\bar\varphi$ the same $\Var(\Theta)$?}
\textbf{Answer: they share the formula, and share the generative structure only under a homogeneity hypothesis that distinct judges violate.} Let judges be conditionally independent given one shared latent $\Theta$, with failure probabilities $p_j(\Theta)$. Then
\[
\Cov(Y_i,Y_j)=\Cov\big(p_i(\Theta),p_j(\Theta)\big),
\]
which equals $\Var(\Theta)$ only when $p_i=p_j=\mathrm{id}$. Exact example (computed). $\Theta\in\{\text{easy},\text{hard}\}$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each. Judge A: $(p_A(\text{easy}),p_A(\text{hard}))=(\tfrac1{10},\tfrac12)$, judge B: $(\tfrac1{10},\tfrac9{10})$, judge C: $(\tfrac3{10},\tfrac3{10})$.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{Homogeneous control} ($p_j=\Theta$ for all $j$): $\varphi$, the Kish-exact $\tilde\rho$ and the symmetrised $\rho_{\mathrm{sym}}$ all equal $\Var(\Theta)/[s(1-s)]$ exactly. So the identity holds in Bay--Yearick's regime.
\item \emph{Heterogeneous (A,B,C):} $\Cov(A,C)=\Cov(B,C)=0$ although all three share $\Theta$, and $\Cov(A,B)=\tfrac2{25}$. Three candidate ``$\rho$''s give three numbers. The mean pairwise correlation is $\bar\varphi\approx0.1164$. The value that makes $1+(J-1)\rho$ exact for the panel mean is $\tilde\rho=\sum_{i\ne j}\Cov_{ij}/((J-1)\sum_i v_i)=\tfrac8{67}\approx0.1194$. The random-relabelling exchangeable value is $\rho_{\mathrm{sym}}=\tfrac{20}{209}\approx0.0957$.
\item \emph{Opposite response} (A with D $=(\tfrac12,\tfrac1{10})$): $\Cov=-\tfrac1{25}<0$ although $\Var(\Theta)>0$.
\item \emph{Fr\'echet cap:} marginals $(\tfrac1{10},\tfrac12)$ allow correlation at most $\tfrac13$. Heterogeneous marginals bound $\varphi$ away from $1$ whatever $\Var(\Theta)$ is.
\end{itemize}
Bay--Yearick say this themselves (\S2.1, \textbf{read}): the variance identity ``needs only the common rate $s$''. In their \S1, Kohli et al.\ are ``the same instrument'' applied ``to different objects''. Their $\rho$ is in fact dominated by the between-\emph{problem} spread $\rho_b$ (\S6.1: $\rho=\rho_b+(1-\rho_b)\rho_w$ with $\hat\rho_w\approx0.0007$), which is a single-model object.
\textbf{Two consequences for the thesis.} (1) The one-line generative statement is correct only with ``\ldots and the judges are exchangeable''. Without that clause, the object is $\Cov(p_i(\Theta),p_j(\Theta))$. That covariance can be zero or negative under a shared latent, which is perhaps the more interesting fact for ``diversity works''. (2) If Kohli's $2.18$ is computed from $\bar\varphi$ (\textbf{recalled}, from your note), it uses an approximation to $\tilde\rho$. The gap is small in my example ($0.1164$ against $0.1194$) but it should be named.

\subsection{\S2: is the per-stratum process really immune?}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{The printed formula.} \S2.2 reads $e_s(n)=1+\lambda(U_s(n)-V_s(n)-\delta_k)$ with $U_s$ an empirical (running) rate. Read literally, this is not a supermartingale. With i.i.d.\ fair $\pm1$ increments and $\lambda=\tfrac12$: $e(1)=\tfrac12$ after $X_1=-1$, but $\E[e(2)\mid X_1=-1]=\tfrac34$ (computed). It is also bounded by $1+\lambda(1+\cdots)$, so a single stratum can never reach $1/\alpha=20$. If the code implements this form, ``Type-I $\approx0.000$'' is automatic and carries no evidence. I expect it is shorthand for $\prod_{m\le n}(1+\lambda(u_m-v_m-\delta_k))$. Please confirm.
\item \textbf{The asymmetry is not established.} ``$\E[V_s]=p_i(\theta_s)p_j(\theta_{t(s)})$ exactly'' uses independence of $W_i$ and $W_j$ for distinct items, given difficulty. That is a hypothesis of the same kind as cross-stratum independence. A persistent, time-varying common cause, your own \S3 mechanism (``model updates propagate simultaneously\ldots''), correlates two items in one stratum shown at different times exactly as it correlates two strata. Either both levels are exposed to drift, or neither is, and the argument that decides it is the same at both levels (\S2.4).
\item \textbf{Adaptedness.} If the derangement $t(s)$ can point to an item that arrives \emph{later}, then $V_s(n)$ is not $\Fc_n$-measurable, and the supermartingale property has no filtration to hold in. Is $t(s)$ always a past item?
\item Minor: \S3 says disjoint bins provide independence. They do not. Disjointness is a design fact, and conditional independence given $\theta$ is a separate assumption (your NOTES-5b line 30, which I have not seen, states it as one).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Vovk--Wang, checked against the source}
\texttt{arXiv:1912.06116}. I reconstructed the counter: \verb|\newtheorem{theorem}{Theorem}[section]| is shared by propositions, so \S3 holds Prop.~3.1 and Thm.~3.2, and \S4 holds Props.~4.1 and 4.2. It suffices that they are \emph{sequential}, $\E[E_k\mid E_1,\dots,E_{k-1}]\le1$, and ``it suffices for $E_1,\dots,E_K$ to be sequential in any order''. That sentence is the key to OQ1. That is ``up to mixing with the constant $1$'', not ``up to scaling''. For example, $cM_K$ with $c>1$ is invalid: take every $E_k\equiv1$.

\subsection{OQ1: well-posedness first}
\emph{``Drift-valid''} must mean: \emph{$E(n)$ is a test supermartingale under a stated composite null with respect to a stated filtration}. A p-value only follows through Ville, $P(\sup_n E(n)\ge1/\alpha)\le\alpha$. So it is a statement about e-processes. The two choices that matter:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Filtration}: the \emph{global} $\Fc_n$ generated by all strata's observations in arrival order, not each stratum's own $\mathcal G^s_n$.
\item \textbf{Null} $H_0^{\mathrm{glob}}$: every per-stratum betting factor satisfies $\E[f_{s,n}\mid\Fc_{n-1}]\le1$. Drift of any kind is allowed, provided it does not make one stratum's excess predictable from the global past.
\end{itemize}
\begin{lemma}[proved: interleaved arrivals]
If each time step updates exactly one stratum, then $\prod_s e_s(n)$ is a single betting process $\prod_{m\le n}f_{s(m),m}$. It is a test supermartingale under $H_0^{\mathrm{glob}}$ with \emph{no} cross-stratum independence: $\E[E(n)\mid\Fc_{n-1}]=E(n-1)\,\E[f_{s(n),n}\mid\Fc_{n-1}]\le E(n-1)$, provided the stratum label $s(n)$ is $\Fc_{n-1}$-predictable or the bound holds conditionally on it.
\end{lemma}
\begin{lemma}[proved and computed: simultaneous updates fail]
If several strata update at the same step from contemporaneous data, validity needs $\E[\prod_s f_{s,n}\mid\Fc_{n-1}]\le1$. This fails under random common shocks even when $H_0^{\mathrm{glob}}$ holds. \textbf{Positive control (exact):} draw a fair $Z_n=\pm1$ each step and set $X_{1,n}=X_{2,n}=Z_n$ with $f=1+\tfrac12X$. Then $\E[f_1f_2\mid\Fc_{n-1}]=\tfrac54$, and $P(\sup_{n\le40}E(n)\ge20)=0.1794>\alpha=0.05$. The joint bet $1+\tfrac14X_1+\tfrac14X_2$ on the same data has step mean $1$ and crossing probability $0.0359\le0.05$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{lemma}[computed: own-filtration validity is not enough]
Use interleaved strata, with one shock persisting across two consecutive steps. Each stratum alone is a martingale in $\mathcal G^s$, but the product has mean $\tfrac54$ per two-step block. $H_0^{\mathrm{glob}}$ is false here, because step $2k$ is predictable from step $2k-1$. That is exactly why it must be the stated null.
\end{lemma}
\begin{lemma}[proved and computed: trends are not correlated increments]
Deterministic common drift (base rates $b_n$ moving together in every stratum) gives product step mean exactly $1$, checked at five drift values. Strata ``moving together'' is not the threat. The threat is \emph{random} common shocks that the past does not predict.
\end{lemma}
\textbf{So OQ1 is well-posed, and its answer is a design choice.} Interleave arrivals and state $H_0^{\mathrm{glob}}$: the product is valid under arbitrary drift (Vovk--Wang's ``sequential in any order''). If you keep simultaneous batches, use \S2.5. The D\&S premise survives only in this conditional, sequential form, which is weaker than independence and is a design property rather than a property of the world.

\subsection{OQ2: a middle construction}
\textbf{As posed}, with $f(e_1,\dots,e_K)$ a symmetric function of the final values and validity required under \emph{arbitrary} dependence, the answer is \textbf{no}: Thm.~3.2 caps $f$ by $\lambda+(1-\lambda)M_K$. A middle construction therefore has to use the time structure.
\begin{lemma}[proved: joint linear bet]
Suppose $|X_{s,n}|\le b$, $\E[X_{s,n}\mid\Fc_{n-1}]\le0$ for each $s$, and $\lambda_{s,n}\ge0$ are $\Fc_{n-1}$-predictable with $\sum_s\lambda_{s,n}\le1/b$. Then $\prod_{m\le n}\big(1+\sum_s\lambda_{s,m}X_{s,m}\big)$ is a test supermartingale under \emph{arbitrary contemporaneous dependence} across strata.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The factor is nonnegative by the constraint, and its conditional mean is $1+\sum_s\lambda_{s,n}\E[X_{s,n}\mid\Fc_{n-1}]\le1$ by linearity. Dependence never enters.
\end{proof}
\textbf{Power} (\texttt{growth.py}, \textbf{floating point}; exact enumeration over $\{\pm1\}^4$, oracle bet sizes, independent strata under the alternative). Rates are expected log-growth per step.

\subsection{The slack $\Delta(\rho)$: bound or definition?}
\emph{Both, depending on which $\rho$ is meant.} With $\rho$ the true covariance, the exact normaliser $1+\lambda^2\Cov(X_1,X_2\mid\Fc_{n-1})$ is a \emph{definition}. It depends on the unknown null law and cannot be used. With $\rho$ estimated from data, validity is lost unless the estimate is predictable \emph{and} the null forces the true covariance below it. The usable version is the worst case. For $|X_s|\le1$ and $\E[X_s\mid\Fc]\le0$,
\[
\E\big[(1+\lambda X_1)(1+\lambda X_2)\mid\Fc\big]\le1+\lambda^2
\]
under any dependence (\textbf{proved}: expand, and use $X_1X_2\le1$). The bound is attained (control above) and never exceeded over the 1010 null-satisfying joint laws on a lattice in $\{-1,0,1\}^2$ (\textbf{computed}). So $(1+\lambda X_1)(1+\lambda X_2)/(1+\lambda^2)$ is valid. It is a bound, but I would use the joint linear bet instead: same validity, no deflation, and none of the deflation compounding over $K$ strata.
