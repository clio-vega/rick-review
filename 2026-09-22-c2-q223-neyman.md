## 1. Summary of the paper in one sentence

Neyman constructs a weight-preserving bijection `CRSK` between pairs of semistandard cylindric
tableaux with a common inner shape and pairs with a common outer shape
(`\label{crsk}`, l.1250 — Thm 5.2), derives from it a cylindric Cauchy identity
(`\label{schurpols}`, l.1677 — Thm 5.14), a symmetry property
(`\label{symmetry}`, l.1739 — Thm 5.18) and their corollaries, and adds a marble-game model, a
transfer principle from cylindric to skew tableaux (§7 `\label{cylskew}`, l.1961), and a
negative observation about cyclic Knuth equivalence (§8, l.2105).

That I can state this in one sentence is itself part of the finding: the paper is entirely
about *bijections between tableaux*, and nowhere about *supports of the resulting polynomials*.

### 2.3 The falsifiable question

My claim has three conjuncts. Asking which one Neyman could falsify:

1. **Support.** `supp(s^c_{λ/μ}(x_1..x_ℓ)) = {α : sort(α) ⊴ λ̂}` with λ̂ the sorted weight of an
   explicit greedy chain (Thm 8.1 of the M-convexity paper). Neyman never forms the support of
   a cylindric skew Schur polynomial. **Untouched.**
2. **M-convexity / SNP.** **Untouched** — the word does not occur, and neither does any
   discrete-convexity vocabulary.
3. **Route.** The registry claim is explicitly a claim about the *route*: a proof that does not
   consume Lam 2008 Cor. 8.5 or Lee 2019 Cor. 5. A source can only scoop a route by *being* an
   alternative route, i.e. by proving M-convexity. Neyman does not prove M-convexity.
   **Cannot reach the claim.**

This is the point I want on the record, because the fear evaluates the *topic* ("someone did
cylindric RSK eleven years ago") while the node records a claim *with its exclusion clause*.
The scary proposition and the recorded one are different propositions.

### 2.4 One near miss that is a name collision, not a scoop

Neyman's §5.3 is titled *The Symmetry Property of CRSK*, and `\label{symmetry}` (l.1739,
Thm 5.18) says: if `CRSK((T,U),μ) = ((P,Q),λ)` then `CRSK((U,T),μ) = ((Q,P),λ)`. My
`cylindric-bender-knuth` node claims Cor. 3.3 reproves *Postnikov's symmetry theorem*, i.e.
that `s^c_{λ/μ}` is a symmetric function of the `x_i`. **These are different statements.**
Neyman's is the transpose-symmetry of a bijection; mine is `S_ℓ`-invariance of a generating
function. Neyman never states, uses, or proves that cylindric skew Schur functions are
symmetric — confirmed by the `symmetric` census above. His Thm 5.14 is stated with *two*
variable sets `x` and `y` precisely so that no symmetry is needed. No overlap.

### 2.5 A correction the browse log already made, which I confirm at source

The 09-22 c1 log recorded, from Dobner's one-line remark, that Thm 5.14 and Cor 5.21 are
"identities involving skew Schur **and** cylindric Schur functions". At source they are
**entirely cylindric**: in `\label{schurpols}` (l.1677) and `\label{oneschurcor}` (l.1824)
every one of α, β, μ, λ ranges over `\Cylpar` and every `s_{·/·}` is a cylindric skew Schur
function. The skew-Schur connection is a *separate* contribution — §7 `\label{cylskew}`
(l.1961), the transfer principle. Dobner's remark merged two results. The c2 browse log had
already caught and recorded this; I verified it independently at source and it is right.

## 3. Verification of the two headline identities

I did not find these checked anywhere, so I checked them. All code in
`reviews/code-2026-09-22-c2/`.

**Instrument validation first.** I implemented `SSCT(λ/μ)` twice from Neyman's §2 definitions
(l.112–250): once by brute-force filling of the fundamental domain with the column-strictness
imposed on the *cylinder* (`gf_direct`), and once by the chain-of-horizontal-strips model
(`gf_chain`), using his footnote criterion `λ_i ≥ μ_i ≥ λ_{i+1}` at l.247. The two agree on
**684/684** instances over `(k,n) ∈ {(1,3),(2,4),(2,5),(3,5)}`, all shapes with ≤ 5 boxes,
`ℓ ≤ 3` (`xcheck.py`). No parameter was fitted.

**Theorem `\label{schurpols}` (l.1677, Thm 5.14), the cylindric Cauchy identity.** Both sides
are infinite sums, so I compared them bidegree by bidegree in `(x, y)`. **494/494** instances
over `(k,n) ∈ {(1,3),(2,4),(2,5),(3,5)}`, all α, β with parts in `[0,2]`, `d_x ≤ 3`, `d_y ≤ 4`.
**0 failures.**

**Corollary `\label{oneschurcor}` (l.1824, Cor 5.21).** **205/205** instances over
`(k,n) ∈ {(1,3),(2,4),(2,5),(3,5),(3,6),(2,6)}`, degrees `d ≤ 4`. **0 failures.**

**One presentational gap, not an error.** Neyman defines `s_{λ/μ}` (l.~229) as "a power series
of **bounded degree**", but the sums in `\label{schurpols}` and `\label{oneschurcor}` run over
*infinitely many* μ (resp. λ): for any α there are infinitely many cylindric μ ⊆ α, since parts
may be arbitrarily negative. The sums are nevertheless well defined, because
`s_{α/μ}(x)` is homogeneous of degree `|α/μ| = Σ_{i<k}(α_i − μ_i)` and for each fixed degree `d`
only finitely many μ contribute. But the identity is therefore one of *formal* power series of
**unbounded** degree, in the completion — which is not the ring the definition names, and the
paper does not say so. This does not threaten either theorem; the bijection is
degree-preserving (stated at l.1697, verbatim: "the bijection takes a pair `(T,U)` to a pair
`(P,Q)` such that `wt(T) = wt(P)` and `wt(U) = wt(Q)`"). It is worth one sentence in any
revision. **Severity: cosmetic, stated as cosmetic — and I have checked the second claim
hidden inside that grading, namely that the sums converge degreewise. They do.**

## 4. Connections to my own work

### 4.1 The dictionary

Neyman's `C_{k,n} = Z²/(−k, n−k)Z` with cylindric partitions weakly decreasing and
`λ_m = λ_{m+k} + (n−k)` (l.116) maps to my `Cyl^{n,m}` of
`proofs/2026-09-20-c1-cylindric-M-convexity.tex` §2 by

> **m := k**, total **n := n**, and **`x_i = λ_{−i} + i`**  (i ∈ Z).

Under this, `x` is strictly increasing with `x_{i+m} = x_i + n`; containment matches
coordinatewise; `|λ/μ|` matches `u(λ) − u(μ)`; and my horizontal-strip condition
`ν_i ≤ ρ_i < ν_{i+1}` is *exactly* Neyman's footnote `λ_{j+1} ≤ μ_j ≤ λ_j` (l.247) after the
index flip. The two conventions are vertical reflections of one another — his rows shorten
downward, mine lengthen downward. Derived and checked in `bridge.py`.

### 4.2 (T1) My main theorem, re-tested in his coordinates

Running my greedy recursion `g^t_i = min(g^{t−1}_{i+1} − 1, λ_i)` through the dictionary and
comparing against the support computed by `gf_chain` (which was written from *Neyman's*
definitions and validated against brute-force filling, §3):

> `supp(s^c_{λ/μ}(x_1..x_ℓ)) = {α ∈ N^ℓ : sort(α) ⊴ λ̂}` — **584/584** instances over
> `(k,n) ∈ {(1,3),(2,4),(2,5),(3,5),(3,6)}`, all λ ⊇ μ with parts in `[0,2]`, `ℓ ≤ 4`.
> **0 failures**, including the empty cases (`T_ℓ = ∅` iff the greedy chain fails to reach λ).

This is a better verification than the one in §9 of the M-convexity paper, because the entire
enumeration side is an independent re-implementation in a different coordinate system, from a
different author's definitions.

### 4.3 (T2) A new corollary: Neyman Cor 5.21 × my Thm 8.1

Restricting `\label{oneschurcor}` (l.1824) to degree `d` and to `ℓ` variables gives an identity
between two *finite* sums of cylindric skew Schur polynomials with non-negative coefficients.
Supports of such a sum are unions of supports, so my Thm 8.1 turns it into a statement about
the greedy statistic alone:

> **Corollary (new).** Fix a cylindric partition α, `d ≥ 0`, `ℓ ≥ 1`. Then the
> dominance-maximal elements of `{ λ̂(α/μ, ℓ) : μ ∈ Cylpar, μ ⊆ α, |α/μ| = d }` and of
> `{ λ̂(λ/α, ℓ) : λ ∈ Cylpar, λ ⊇ α, |λ/α| = d }` coincide.

Checked on **812/812** instances over `(k,n) ∈ {(1,3),(2,4),(2,5),(3,5),(3,6),(2,6),(4,6)}`,
`d ≤ 4`, `ℓ ≤ 4`; **341** of them had more than one distinct λ̂ on a side, so the statement is
not generically vacuous. **0 failures.**

**Honesty about what this check is and is not.** It is a *consequence* of two things I already
believe, so it **could not have failed** unless my implementation or the dictionary were wrong.
It is therefore a test of the instrument, not of the mathematics, and I am not recording it as
independent confirmation of Thm 8.1. What it *is* worth is as a statement: a purely
order-theoretic symmetry of the greedy chain about α, under the reflection that swaps "remove
`d` boxes from α" with "add `d` boxes to α", which I would not have guessed and cannot yet
prove directly. **Opened as Q225** (§8).

### 4.4 Two things in Neyman worth my attention later

- **§7 `\label{cylskew}` (l.1961), the transfer principle.** Embed a skew shape in a cylinder
  with both periods large relative to the degree `d`, prove the cylindric statement, read off
  the degree-`d` homogeneous component; explicit bounds `k = max(m,p) + 2d + 1`,
  `n = k + max(a_1,b_1) + 2d + 1`. Neyman is scrupulous that his demonstration is not new. But
  the *direction* is the interesting one for me: it says a cylindric support theorem specialises
  to a skew one. My Thm 8.1 is a cylindric support theorem. What it gives back in the skew case
  under this transfer is worth one session.
- **§8 (l.2105), an eleven-year-old open problem.** Naive cyclic Knuth equivalence is useless
  because *all* cylindric tableaux of the same weight fall into one class; Neyman writes that a
  non-collapsing variant "would be an important development in cylindric tableau theory". Note
  the shape of the collapse: the natural equivalence is exactly weight-classifying, i.e. it
  discards precisely the support invariant that my theorem computes. I do not claim this is a
  route; I record that the obstruction and my object are complementary.

## 6. Second item — the (G1) exhaustive null. **This is the strongest finding of the session.**

`proofs/2026-09-22-c1-crystal-edges-are-exchange-moves.tex` §(G1) grades the `X = ∅` case
*computational, not proved*, and names its obstruction:

> "Theorem `thm:contain`(iii) invokes Lemma `lem:step1`, which needs `(S,T)` additive, whereas
> the `X = ∅` construction pairs the **reduced** pair `(S∖{b−1}, T∖{b})`, **which need not be
> additive**. […] it holds in all 1636 instances with `n ≤ 7`."

The brief asked the right question: *does `n ≤ 7` cross the regime in which that failure can
exist at all?* It does not — because **the failure does not exist**.

Over every additive pair `(S,T)` with `X(S,T) = ∅` and every Case-(3) index `b`
(`g1_reach.py`; my `emptyX.case3_bs`, lengths by Shi's inversion formula on window notation):

| `n` | additive pairs, `X = ∅` | `(pair, b)` cases | reduced pair **non**-additive |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 9 | 9 | **0** |
| 4 | 34 | 36 | **0** |
| 5 | 115 | 130 | **0** |
| 6 | 365 | 444 | **0** |
| 7 | 1113 | 1463 | **0** |
| 8 | 3306 | 4704 | **0** |

(The `n ≤ 7` pair counts sum to 1636, matching the paper.) I then dropped the Case-(3)
hypothesis entirely and tested the general bracketed deletion (`g1_lemma.py`):

> **Conjecture (bracketed-pair deletion).** If `(S,T)` is additive, `i ∈ S` and `i+1 ∈ T`, then
> `(S∖{i}, T∖{i+1})` is additive.
>
> **82044/82044** instances for `3 ≤ n ≤ 8`. **0 failures.**

And — the part that stops this from being another exhaustive null that bounds the search rather
than the question — the *unbracketed* version is **false, from `n = 3`** (`g1_lemma2.py`):
additivity is **not** hereditary under `S' ⊆ S`, `T' ⊆ T`; 412578 failures out of 1822537
instances for `n ≤ 7`, smallest witness `n = 3`, `S = {0}`, `T = {0,1}`, `S' = {0}`,
`T' = {0}`. So the bracketed statement is a genuine lemma about the pairing, not a triviality
that any subset would satisfy, and the search range does cross the regime where it could fail.

**Consequences, stated precisely.**

1. The sentence "which need not be additive" in (G1) is an **unverified assertion, and it
   appears to be false.** This is the second, hidden claim inside a gap grading — the gap says
   *not proved*, which is honest, and then says *how it behaves*, which was never checked. That
   second clause should be withdrawn.
2. The 1636-instance null in §`sec:emptyX` **did verify its conclusion** (the moves lie in
   `E^mv`). What it did *not* do is test the named obstruction, because the obstruction never
   arose. I want that distinction on the record rather than a blanket "the null was vacuous".
3. **(G1) is probably closable, and there is a concrete route:** prove the bracketed-pair
   deletion conjecture; then `lem:step1` applies to the reduced pair and `thm:contain`(iii)
   goes through verbatim in the `X = ∅` case, upgrading §`sec:emptyX` from *computed* to
   *proved*. The natural attack is the length-additivity criterion Lem. 2.3 of
   `2026-09-21-c1-affine-stanley-exchange.tex`, the same tool `lem:step1` already uses.
   **Opened as Q224** (§8). I recommend this as the next PROVE brief; it is a small, sharp,
   apparently-true lemma with a named consequence.
