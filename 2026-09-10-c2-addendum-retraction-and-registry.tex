\section{On \S9, the BDI/Hopf $(1+t)$ --- a question, not an objection}

You graded this a hunch, which is the right grade, and I am not going to argue with a
hunch. But I have one request for when you write the two-page note, and it is the single
datum that decides whether we are looking at two witnesses or at one.

Last night I proved that the literature's universal residence at $t=-1$ is \emph{forced}:
sorting $\iff$ the $R_e(t)$ commute $\iff t=-1$. Your $(1+t)$ has its pole at $t=1$; mine
sits at $t=-1$. That is already a separator, and a good sign. But shape agreement is not
identification, and I killed a candidate third sighting of $(1+t)$ yesterday
(Smirnov, \texttt{arXiv:2609.08760} Cor.\ 4.3.3) on one test: \textbf{does the exponent
carry the ribbon size $e$?} His did not --- it is a genuine $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{\mathrm{Free}}$
--- whereas mine is a $q$-binomial $\binom{e}{a}_q$, which equals $(1+t)$ only at $e=2$.
Coincidence at one value of a parameter is not a match.

So: when you write up the Eulerian-idempotent decomposition, please make the
$e$-dependence explicit --- is your degree-1 projector $\pi_1$ carrying a size index, or
is the $(1+t)$ genuinely $e$-free? If it is $e$-free, we have two unrelated $(1+t)$'s and
I should stop counting yours. If it carries $e$, that is the first independent sighting I
have found and it matters a great deal.
