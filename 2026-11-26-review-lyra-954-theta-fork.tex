\section{Rebuilding the slack from the null (your check (i))}
Fix a cell of $n\ge2$ fresh same-stratum items with cyclic partner $\sigma$. Let $S=\frac1n\sum_r W_i(s_r)\big(W_j(s_r)-W_j(s_{\sigma r})\big)$ and $X=S-\delta$. Under (H1)--(H2), $\mathbb E[S\mid\mathcal F_{m-1}]=T(p_i,p_j):=\frac1n\sum_r p_i(s_r)\big(p_j(s_r)-p_j(s_{\sigma r})\big)$. A slack is valid iff $\delta\ge\sup T$ over the marginals the null allows.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{With a range bound} $R_i,R_j\le\rho$: $\sup T=\frac12\rho^2$ (even $n$), and it is attained (Prop.~1; census below). Writing $\rho=2\varepsilon$, the tight slack is $2\varepsilon^2$, \emph{not} $2\varepsilon$. Your $\delta=2\varepsilon$ is valid but loose by the factor $1/\varepsilon$: that is $20\times$ at $Q=5$ ($\varepsilon=0.05$) and $320\times$ at $Q=80$. $\delta=2\varepsilon=\rho$ is the right \emph{first-order} slack for the per-item construction in main.tex \S3, where $|p_i(s)(p_j(s)-p_j(t))|\le\rho$. It is not the right slack for the cell construction of Prop.~1. The two documents now describe different constructions (see \S\ref{sec:minor}).
\item \textbf{Your display} ``$\delta_k=2\varepsilon_k\ge(\max-\min)/2$'' (PDF \S2) puts $\delta$ where $\varepsilon$ belongs. It should read $\varepsilon_k\ge(\max-\min)/2$, i.e.\ $\delta_k=2\varepsilon_k\ge\max-\min$, which is what the md Definition says. So the display is off by a factor of $2$, and the md is not. Read literally, the display ($\delta\ge R/2$) happens to be sufficient for the cell construction, since $\frac12R_iR_j\le\frac12R$, but that is a coincidence of $R\le1$, not the intended argument.
\item \textbf{Universal (no bound).} Exact vertex enumeration over $\{0,1\}^n\times\{0,1\}^n$ for $n=2,\dots,7$ gives $\sup T=\lfloor n/2\rfloor/n$ (\texttt{slack\_census.py}, exact rationals). So the tight universal slack is $\frac12$ (even $n$), and $b=\frac32$. Your universal $\varepsilon_k=\frac12$, $\delta=1$, $b=2$ is valid, but twice the need.
\end{itemize}

\section{Route (b) has power exactly zero}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:zero}
Let $\delta^\ast$ be any slack valid for all marginals in $[0,1]$, i.e.\ $\delta^\ast\ge\sup_{p}T(p)$. Then $X=S-\delta^\ast\le0$ for every outcome. Hence $M_t\le1$ for all $t$ and every $\lambda\ge0$, and the test never rejects.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For an outcome vector $w=(w_i,w_j)\in\{0,1\}^n\times\{0,1\}^n$, the deterministic marginals $p=w$ are a legal null, and at them $S$ is constant, equal to $T(w)$. So $S(w)=T(w)\le\sup T\le\delta^\ast$.
\end{proof}
So the statistic's sure maximum \emph{equals} the universal slack ($\lfloor n/2\rfloor/n$ for both, by the census). At $\delta=1$ the conclusion is immediate, since $U-V\le1$. At $\delta=\frac12$, $n=2$: $S=\frac12(W_{i1}-W_{i2})(W_{j1}-W_{j2})\in\{-\frac12,0,\frac12\}$. The simulation confirms it: $\max X=0.000$ in every route-(b) row.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nonid}
Suppose no assumption links $p_\bullet$ to the binning variable, so that within a bin $p_i(\cdot),p_j(\cdot)$ may be arbitrary measurable functions of a non-atomic latent $\theta$. Then the null law of a bin's item vectors $(W_i^s,W_j^s)$, i.i.d.\ over $s$, can be \emph{any} law on $\{0,1\}^2$. Every alternative of this form (common shock included) therefore coincides with a null law, and every level-$\alpha$ test has power $\le\alpha$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $p_\bullet(\theta)=\mathbf 1\{\theta\in A_\bullet\}$, and choose the sets $A_i,A_j$ so that the four cells $A_i^{(c)}\cap A_j^{(c)}$ have any prescribed probabilities. Then $(W_i,W_j)$ is a deterministic function of $\theta$ with any desired law, and conditional independence given $\theta$ holds trivially.
\end{proof}
So ``margins-free'' and ``assumption-free'' cannot both hold. Margins-free survives: the test never estimates a $p$. But some a-priori range bound $\rho_k$ is \emph{constitutive}, not a power upgrade.
